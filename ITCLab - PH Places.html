About El Nido

El Nido is a coastal town in the northern part of Palawan, in the Philippines. It sits beside Bacuit Bay, where dozens of islands rise straight out of clear water. Tall limestone cliffs, coral reefs, and quiet white-sand beaches make it one of the most visited destinations in the country. The name comes from the Spanish word for "the nest," after the edible swiftlet nests once gathered from the cliffs here.

Attractions

Five places that show El Nido at its best.

Big Lagoon

Big Lagoon is a calm, emerald-green lagoon on Miniloc Island. Tall limestone cliffs rise straight out of the water. The shallow, clear water makes it popular for kayaking and paddleboarding.

Nacpan Beach

Nacpan Beach is a long golden-sand beach stretching several kilometers along El Nido's coast. It is lined with palm trees and has a relaxed, quiet atmosphere. It is a favorite spot for swimming and watching the sunset.

Snake Island

Snake Island is named for the curving white sandbar that winds through shallow water like a snake. The sandbar is most visible at low tide. It is a popular stop on island-hopping tours, with clear water ideal for walking and snorkeling.

Hidden Beach

Hidden Beach is a small, secluded beach on Matinloc Island, tucked between steep limestone cliffs. It is reached through a narrow opening in the rocks, which gives it its name. The calm water and peaceful setting make it a favorite for swimming and photos.

Las Cabañas Beach

Las Cabañas Beach is a short ride from El Nido town and is known for its sunsets. Visitors relax on the sand with a drink as the sky turns orange, and the nearby shore is good for a quiet swim.

Activities

Things to do at these places and around Bacuit Bay.

Island hopping

Boat tours visit lagoons, beaches, and sandbars in one day. Big Lagoon, Snake Island, and Hidden Beach are common stops.

Kayaking and paddleboarding

Paddle across the shallow water of Big Lagoon and glide beside the limestone cliffs.

Snorkeling and diving

Reefs around the bay are home to colorful fish and corals. Dive shops in town offer lessons and guided dives.

Swimming and sunsets

Swim at Nacpan Beach, then stay for the sunset at Las Cabañas.

Hiking

Climb Taraw Cliff for wide views over the town and the bay.

Photography

Hidden Beach, the Snake Island sandbar, and the cliff-lined lagoons make for great photos.

Food and Culture

El Nido's food comes from the sea, and its culture comes from the people of Palawan.

Fresh seafood

Grilled fish, squid, prawns, and crab are sold fresh in town restaurants and on the beach. Many places let you pick your own catch and choose how it is cooked.

Kinilaw

Raw fish cured in vinegar or calamansi with onion, ginger, and chili. It is a Filipino favorite on the coast.

Palawan cashews

Cashew nuts are a well-known product of Palawan and a popular pasalubong (souvenir) to bring home.

Filipino comfort food

Adobo, sinigang, and lechon appear on many menus, served with rice. Finish with halo-halo on a hot afternoon.

Local communities

Many families in the area have fished these waters for generations. Palawan is also home to indigenous groups such as the Tagbanua and Cuyonon, who keep their own languages and traditions.

Respect the place

Take your trash with you, avoid touching corals, use reef-safe sunscreen, and ask before photographing people.

Travel Guide

How to get there

  • Fly to Puerto Princesa, then take a van to El Nido (about 5 to 6 hours).
  • Or take a direct flight to El Nido's Lio Airport, offered by some airlines from Manila.

Best time to visit

The dry season, from about November to May, has the calmest seas and the clearest water. The rainy season can cancel boat tours.

Island-hopping tours

Tours are grouped as Tour A, B, C, and D, each visiting different islands and lagoons. Book through your hotel or a licensed operator in town.

Getting around

Tricycles and motorbike rentals are the main ways to reach beaches such as Nacpan and Las Cabañas.

What to bring

  • Cash, since ATMs can run out
  • Reef-safe sunscreen
  • Water shoes and a dry bag
  • Light, quick-dry clothes

Good to know

Fees, schedules, and prices change, so confirm them with your tour operator or the local tourism office before you travel.

Accommodations

Pick an area that matches your trip.

El Nido town

Close to restaurants, tour offices, and the port. Best for first-time visitors and budget travelers, with hostels and guesthouses.

Corong-Corong and Las Cabañas

A quieter stretch of coast with mid-range resorts and sunset views, a short ride from town.

Nacpan and Calitang

Beachfront cottages and small lodges for a slow, peaceful stay far from the town crowds.

Island resorts

Luxury resorts on private islands in the bay, with boat transfers and all-inclusive options.

Events

Dates change each year, so check with the local tourism office.

Taraw Festival

A local festival in El Nido named after Taraw Cliff, with street dancing, music, and community activities.

Holy Week

Many Filipinos travel during this period, so book rooms and boats early.

Christmas and New Year

A busy season with a festive mood in town and good weather for island hopping.

Town fiesta

Parades, food, and music celebrate the town's patron saint and bring the community together.
